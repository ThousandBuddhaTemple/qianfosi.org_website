About Us

Introduction to Mass Bodhi Siksa Society

The Mass Bodhi Siksa Society was founded in 1990 by Master Kuan Hsien, a disciple of the Hong Kong Buddhist leader Elder Yongxing. It is a traditional Chinese Buddhist monastery established in Quincy, Boston, Massachusetts, and is a non-profit Buddhist charitable organization registered with the U.S. government.

The purpose of this society is to promote traditional Chinese Buddhist monasteries and to benefit all living beings by serving the public. The Thousand Buddha Temple, which belongs to this society, is a place for the public to enhance their Buddhist faith and to practice and study the Dharma together.

The Thousand Buddha Temple holds irregular Buddhist lectures to improve believers' understanding of Buddhist teachings. It regularly leads weekly group practice sessions, guiding the public in chanting sutras, meditation, and other practices, as well as studying and discussing the Dharma.

A library has been opened, which contains various classics in both Chinese and English, as well as Buddhist CDs and audio tapes for free distribution. The society also publishes the quarterly "Bodhi Lun Yin".

A Buddhist children's guidance class and a hospice care chanting group have been established. At the same time, the society has also set up a welfare department to accept applications for student grants, assist families in need, visit and comfort the elderly, and support various disaster relief efforts around the world by donating funds to help victims rebuild their homes.

This society is a Buddhist public organization with the aim of promoting the Buddhist spirit of compassion and salvation, benefiting the public, serving society, and purifying people's hearts. We welcome those who are interested to join our group and contribute their efforts to promoting Buddhism, adorning the land, and benefiting all sentient beings.

Venerable Kuan Hsien's Biography

1932.9.1

Birth

Originally from Guangdong Province, China, born on September 1, 1932.

1977

Took Refuge

In 1977, took refuge in the Triple Gem under Master Yongxing, the president of the Hong Kong Bodhi Siksa Society and abbot of Western Monastery. In the same year, joined the Bodhi Siksa Society and participated in the Buddhist studies seminar organized by the society.

1982.10

Ordination

In October 1982, was ordained as a monk under Master Yongxing at the Western Monastery of the Bodhi Siksa Society.

1983.12

Received Precepts

In December 1983, received the full precepts under the Elder Baisheng at Linji Monastery in Taiwan.

1984

Served as General Affairs Manager

In 1984, by order of his master, he served as the general affairs manager at the Master Xuyun Memorial Hall on Furong Mountain in Tsuen Wan, Hong Kong.

1995

Monastic Practice

Ordained at a monastery in Taiwan, officially beginning his life as a monk.

1985

Served as Alms-bowl Holder

In 1985, returned to serve at the Western Monastery, holding the position of alms-bowl holder.

1988

First Visit to Boston

In 1988, he traveled to Boston, Massachusetts, and learned that there were many Chinese people and institutions of higher learning in the area, but there was a lack of a proper Buddhist monastery. This sparked the idea of building a monastery to provide a clean and solemn place for more devotees to practice together.

1990

Established the Mass Bodhi Siksa Society

In 1990, together with fellow believers and students, he established the Mass Bodhi Siksa Society in Quincy, Massachusetts, and named it Jingguang Temple.

1994

Planned the Thousand Buddha Temple

Later, as the number of believers grew daily, he planned the construction of the Thousand Buddha Temple in Quincy, Massachusetts, in 1994.

2006

Bodhi Buddhist Cultural and Educational Center

At the end of 2006, the Bodhi Buddhist Cultural and Educational Center was completed, which also includes a children's center and a library.
